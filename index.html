About Me

I am a third-year PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Maryland. My research, guided by my advisors Leo Lampropoulos and Ian Miers, explores Programming Languages and Cryptography, with a focus on using formal methods and zero-knowledge proofs to verify private systems without exposing sensitive details.

As an undergrad at Seton Hall University, I worked with Marco Morazán on Automata Theory, extending domain-specific languages with tools to better understand nondeterminism.

I played Division I tennis for Seton Hall University and later for the University of Maryland. Reach out if you'd like to play!

PhD Projects

  • Cryptographic PersonasePrint
    Pseudonyms for your group chat, minus the chaos. Switch between unlinkable personas to speak freely, but get banned on one and you’re banned on all of them. Nobody ever learns who you are; you just lose the privilege of being mysterious. Already runs inside Signal, no server changes required.
  • Zerouette
    Zero-knowledge, meet choreography. A language for writing zero-knowledge proofs that keeps track of what’s secret (and proves in Rocq that secrets never slip out), then compiles to Noir circuits so multi-party protocols can pirouette together without stepping on anyone’s privacy.
  • Shadow Graphs
    Follow the money without ever seeing the money. Private cryptocurrencies hide who paid whom, which is great until something gets stolen. Shadow Graphs lets a theft report chase the funds hop by hop and freeze them wherever they land, and nobody ever sees the full transaction graph.
  • [Title placeholder: Stateful zk‑creds]New
    Anonymous credentials with a memory: combine endorsements from multiple sources into one private, updatable credential, then prove only what’s needed…
  • [Title placeholder: Ideal Functionality Choreographic DSL]New
    Ideal functionalities spell out what a crypto protocol is supposed to do, and they’re easy to get subtly wrong. This DSL lets you write them as choreographies: simple to code up, and easy to test for the properties you actually meant…
